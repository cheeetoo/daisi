Mission

Why a group like this should exist at Dartmouth, and what we mean by AI safety.

We think advanced AI may be one of the most consequential technologies of this century, and that whether it goes well is still an open question. The systems are improving faster than our ability to understand or steer them, and many of the decisions that will shape how this goes are being made now, by a fairly small number of people.

By AI safety we mean the work of making sure increasingly capable systems do what we intend, that we can tell when they don't, and that the institutions around them are ready. That includes technical research on alignment, interpretability and evaluations, and it includes policy and governance. We care most about the failure modes that would be very hard to recover from.

The field is young and small relative to what is at stake. Many of the people doing its best work started within the last few years, often while they were students, and there is far more useful work than there are people to do it. Dartmouth doesn't yet have a community for this. We'd like it to.

DAISI was founded in [term, year] by [names], with support from [Kairos's Pathfinder program]. We run a reading group each term, bring speakers to campus, and help students find their next step, whether that's a research fellowship, a course, or just the right thing to read.

We try to be honest about what is and isn't known, to welcome disagreement, and to make what we run useful rather than just interesting. Nothing we do has prerequisites.
